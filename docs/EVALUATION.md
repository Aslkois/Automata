# Starter roster and Configure evaluation

Generated by `scripts/evaluate.ts` from 8970 simulated battles (892 s on 4 cores). Nothing here is estimated: every figure is counted from real simulation runs with the shipped brains.

**Method.** Battles end when one side is combat-ineffective (disabled, disarmed with no way to fight, or destroyed) or at the 120 s time limit (a draw). A win scores 1, a draw ½. Arenas: Foundry Yard, Open ground, Pillars (Foundry Yard has a central block and four smaller pieces of cover; Open ground has none; Pillars has seven blocks). Each seed (1, 2, 3) uses a different pair of starting positions, and every pairing is played from both sides.

Runtime errors in any brain: **0** (none).

## 8. Investigations

*Written by hand from diagnostic runs (timelines, hit tallies), not generated. The figures quoted were measured during development and may differ slightly from the tables above, which are the current run.*

### What the diagnostics found, and what was changed

- **Drones jammed in wall corners.** In the first round robin, camera-only starters that saw nothing drove into a wall corner and stayed there for the rest of the battle: the front distance sensor read 13 u, the bumper had not fired, and a wheeled drone cannot turn while pushing into a wall. `steerClear` now backs out when the way ahead is blocked within 45 u, and the camera-only starters search with a purpose (the arena middle, a waypoint loop, an orbit) instead of wandering.
- **Every moving shooter aimed wrong.** Rounds leave the muzzle carrying the shooter’s velocity, but `drone.lead` solved the intercept as if the shooter stood still. A Gnat orbiting at 140 u/s landed 16 of 127 autocannon rounds on a Longbow. The lead is now solved in the shooter’s frame. This is a fix for every brain, generated or hand-written, not a change for one starter.
- **Broadside strafing lost duels.** Rapier first strafed across the enemy’s line of fire and scored 7% in its round robin. Armour is one thickness per section, so side-on it showed 12 mm and 8 mm plate instead of its 28 mm front; hit tallies showed 27 penetrations of its middle section in one duel. Rapier was rewritten to fight nose-on, moving by driving forward and reversing — 46% in the same test. The same finding later fixed Configure (below).
- **Light weapons on light frames.** Gnat (one autocannon and a machine gun) scored 8%: its 26 mm autocannon could not get through a 28 mm front, and it took 68 hits for every 15 it landed. Two autocannons on a 12 mm frame took it to 42% in development runs. Jackal’s scatter cannons did very little damage (pellet damage falls with the square of the pellet’s speed, which drops fast) and its 15 mm nose was penetrated at will; a 28 mm medium nose and an autocannon to soften targets took it from about 10% to 32%.
- **Hornet rarely fired.** Its forward camera lost the target in every turn and the state machine fell back to searching; in one 80 s duel it fired six rockets. It now remembers the last sighting and has a RETURN state; it still loses most duels (its plate is thin and its pod reloads for 4.5 s), but a full salvo does real damage when it lands.
- **Warden was a heavy brawler that also intercepted.** With a dual autocannon it scored 88% and beat almost everything at close range. Its offence was reduced to one 26 mm autocannon, matching its role: about 71% in development runs, still the best answer to missiles and rockets.
- **Juggernaut almost never loses a duel** (one loss in 252 in the current run). Hit tallies showed its directional shield absorbing 145 of Paragon’s ~160 hits and its rear quad machine gun stripping opponents’ turrets until they were disarmed. It now carries a compact shield, one tail machine gun, and 55/30/12 mm of plate instead of 70/45/12, so its sides and back are genuinely weak. Its duel score barely moved (87–89%): two braced heavy cannons kill anything that cannot penetrate 55 mm before it gets round. Flankers do not get behind it — Viper’s timeline shows the walker pivoting on the spot to keep its front on Viper, and one shell taking Viper’s front section from 100% to 27%. It can be held to a draw (Citadel, Longbow, Warden, Bulldog) and it can lose to two drones at once. This is reported as a limitation rather than tuned further.
- **Configure circled with its thin side out.** Configure’s Balanced doctrine orbited by default on wheeled drones — the same broadside mistake. In the first full evaluation the default generated brain scored 11% against Picket’s own hand-written brain on Picket’s hardware and 13% against the roster on Kestrel’s. Automatic movement now keeps a front-armoured ground drone nose-on (kiting instead of orbiting) when its front plate is at least 1.5× its other sections. In the current run the same default brains score 39% (Picket), 61% (Rapier) and 58% (Kestrel) against those starters’ own hand-written brains, and Configure’s mean against the roster rose from 48% to 51%.

### How Sovereign was engineered

Sovereign was developed by measurement, not by stacking equipment. The figures are from development runs (Sovereign against the hardest starters and the custom opponents, 252 battles per candidate) and differ from the final tables above.

- **The chassis.** At about 15 t with 70 mm on every section, tracks reach 113 u/s and turn on the spot; the same hull on legs managed 72 u/s. Tracks it is.
- **70 mm everywhere, not just in front.** Armour is one thickness per section. 70 mm stops every missile (65 mm), rocket (36 mm) and autocannon round (50 mm); only heavy cannons and coilguns get through.
- **Nose-on beats angling.** The first brain held the hull 45° to heavy guns, reasoning that 70 mm at 45° is ~99 mm on both faces. Measured against the heavy opponents it was worse: 95% against 99% nose-on (Dreadnought 81% against 97%). Angled, the long sides of all three sections — including the middle one, which holds the CPU — are exposed; nose-on, only the narrow front can be hit. The brain fights nose-on.
- **Shells explode.** A shell rack holds 20 shells and cooks off when hit (9 per shell). In one trial a single hit on a forward rack wrecked the front section’s cooling, autoloader and point defense in the first seconds; in another, a rack in the middle section took the power core with it. Every shell now rides in the rear section, which faces away from the fight, and a spall liner guards the middle.
- **Heavy cannons over coilguns.** Coilguns are accurate, cannot be shot down and their rounds do not cook off — but a four-coilgun hull scored 93% and only 42% against the heavy-cannon battleship Dreadnought, against 99% and 97% for four heavy cannons. Damage per second decided it.
- **Enough shells for a long fight.** With two racks, all four cannons ran dry 150 s into a fight against two Juggernauts (it still won, on its autocannon and rockets). A third rack (60 shells) fixed it.
- **Active defence matters against missiles.** Missiles cannot get through 70 mm, but their blasts strip turrets, sensors and tracks. The first hull, with one point-defense gun and no interceptors, was stripped and stuck in a corner by Hydra. Point defense plus an interceptor pod and cassette answer it.
- **A bad brain wastes the hardware.** The first brain scored 6% against Dreadnought on the first hull; the same hull with the second brain scored 63–83%. The decisive fixes: firing full volleys as soon as a gun has a solution (the first brain staggered and waited for a still hull); wall-following detours round cover (it had sat for the whole battle 250 u from an enemy hidden behind the central block); a 1.2 s grace before treating a target that flickers behind a corner as lost (the plan had flipped between engage and pursue every decision); going round beside hidden enemies instead of into their cover; and keeping the turrets on where the enemy should appear, so the first volley is ready.
- **A broken sensor is not a wall.** Mirror matches against Configure-generated brains on the same hull exposed a bug: when the front distance sensor was shot off, `openDistance` read 0, the brain took that for a wall ahead and reversed for the rest of the battle. It now checks `rangeStatus` and, on a side whose sensor is gone, steers by the bumper and its memory, and says so in the log.
- **Stand and fire.** The brain closed to 450 u before settling. Tracked hulls throw their guns off while moving, so every second spent closing was a second without a good solution. It now stops and fires anywhere inside 850 u at its default reach, and only closes when its remembered failures say mid range is not working.
- **Interceptors on shells.** Heavy-cannon shells are slow enough to be intercepted, and the generated brains spent their interceptor rockets on them while Sovereign kept its own for missiles that, against a battleship, never came. It now holds two in reserve only after it has seen missiles, and otherwise spends spare rockets on incoming shells.
- **How much of it is the hull?** Most of it, measured honestly. Configure’s four doctrines on Sovereign’s exact hardware scored 92–99% against the same 252-battle hard set where Sovereign’s own brain scored 99% (250 wins, 2 draws); interception was the best generated brain. Against packs of two or three drones the gap was wider but still modest: Sovereign’s brain averaged 86% over eight packs, the best generated brains 81–82%. Where the hand-written brain shows is in finishing fights (the generated brains draw far more on time against the battleships and the missile ship) and in packs, where its threat model also uses what each enemy has shown it can do (incoming rounds traced back to their shooter) and it holds one target until a clearly worse threat appears; the generated brains judge threat from size, distance, heading and closing speed alone. In mirror matches it is not better: over the four doctrines it scored 47% in 72 duels. Those duels are decided in the first two seconds by which side loses its dual autocannon or a front cannon first. Loosening its heavy-gun aim tolerance to fire sooner changed none of the 72 results (the turrets slew through the threshold within one decision) and cost three draws on the hard set, so it was not kept.
- **Two battleships win.** No brain on this hull — Sovereign’s or any of Configure’s — reliably beats two Dreadnoughts at once (development runs: 0–17% over six battles each). Each Dreadnought carries the same four heavy cannons behind the same 70 mm; together they put twice Sovereign’s weight of fire on its front, which fails in 20–45 s. At best Sovereign takes one with it (on the pillars arena, in two of six). Beating them would take something the rules do not offer a single machine: more armour than 70 mm, or a way to fight them one at a time that the open arenas do not provide. This is reported as the limit rather than tuned against.
